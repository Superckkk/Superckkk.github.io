## 阅读 · Reading {module: reading}

**Read the passage and answer the questions.**

**What does a pair of trainers really cost?**

Mia, fourteen, saved her pocket money for two months to buy a pair of trainers. The price tag said £90. In the shop, it felt like a lot of money. In the factory where they were made, it looks very different.

Most trainers are made in Vietnam or Indonesia, where factory workers earn about £12 a day. One worker sews the parts of roughly one hundred pairs of shoes in a week.

So where do the £90 go? About £15 pays for the materials — the rubber, cloth and glue. The workers who cut and sew the shoes get about £4 per pair. Shipping the shoes by boat to Britain costs about £3. The shop in London pays around £25 for rent and staff. Around £10 goes to advertising — the posters, the famous athletes on TV. The brand keeps the rest, about £33, as profit.

Are the brands the bad guys? Not so fast. Customers want cheap shoes, and £4 of labour is a small part of the final price. In fact, if the brand doubled the workers' pay, the price of the trainers would only rise by about £4. Many shoppers say they care about workers, but when two pairs look the same, most people still buy the cheaper one.

There is good news. More young people now buy second-hand trainers online, and some brands now show exactly where their shoes are made. Mia wears her £90 trainers every day — and she has decided that her next pair will be second-hand.
